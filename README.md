### AI-Assisted Material Selection for Engineering, Manufacturing & Product Aesthetics

**Domain:** Materials Engineering · Materials Informatics · AI · Manufacturing · Product Design

## 📌 Overview

**MATSEL PRO** is an AI-assisted material selection and aesthetic intelligence platform developed to integrate **engineering requirements, manufacturing feasibility, cost, sustainability and product aesthetics** into a unified material-selection workflow.

Traditional engineering material selection primarily focuses on quantitative properties such as mechanical performance, thermal behaviour and chemical resistance. However, product development also depends heavily on factors such as:

- Surface finish
- Visual appearance
- Material perception
- Manufacturing process
- Cost
- Production volume
- Sustainability
- Application requirements
- Product aesthetics

MATSEL PRO attempts to bridge this gap by connecting **materials engineering with aesthetic design intelligence**.

The platform combines a structured materials database with:

- Intelligent material recommendation
- Multi-criteria scoring
- Manufacturing process planning
- Surface-finish perception mapping
- Parametric cost estimation
- Vision-based image analysis
- AI-generated engineering explanations

The overall workflow is:

```text
Product Requirements
        ↓
Material Database
        ↓
Multi-Criteria Recommendation
        ↓
Manufacturing Process
        ↓
Surface Finish
        ↓
Cost Estimation
        ↓
Aesthetic Evaluation
        ↓
AI-Assisted Design Decision
```

# 🎯 Problem Statement

Material selection is not simply a matter of finding a material with sufficient strength.

A practical product-design decision often needs to satisfy several constraints simultaneously:

```text
Mechanical Performance
        +
Manufacturing Feasibility
        +
Cost
        +
Sustainability
        +
Surface Finish
        +
Aesthetic Requirements
        +
Application Requirements
```

Conventional material-selection databases generally provide engineering properties, while aesthetic decisions are often made separately by designers.

This creates a gap between:

```text
Engineering Specification
          ↓
Material Selection
          ↓
Manufacturing Process
          ↓
Surface Finish
          ↓
Visual Appearance
          ↓
User Perception
```

MATSEL PRO was developed to computationally connect these stages.

The project specifically investigates how measurable parameters such as **surface roughness (Ra)** and **optical gloss (GU)** can act as a bridge between engineering specifications and aesthetic perception. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

# 📐 Recommendation Scoring

The scoring engine uses five major components.

## 1. Mechanical Score

The mechanical component penalises materials with high minimum achievable surface roughness.

---

## 2. Aesthetic Score

The system parses the gloss range using regular expressions, calculates an average value and normalises the result.

---

## 3. Cost Score

The system compares minimum material cost against the user's target budget.

Progressive penalties are applied when materials become expensive relative to the specified budget.

---

## 4. Sustainability Score

Sustainability-related information is identified using keyword matching within the database notes.

---

## 5. Compliance Bonus

A bonus is applied when the material's typical applications match relevant industry standards triggered by the user's selected application.

---

# 🏆 Material Ranking

The recommendation engine evaluates the available materials and ranks them according to the calculated total score.

The report describes the system as evaluating all **850 materials** and returning the **top six candidates**.

Each candidate is then connected to compatible manufacturing processes. :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}

# 💰 Module 4 — Cost Estimation Engine

Economic viability is incorporated through a parametric cost model.

The total unit cost is decomposed into:

```text
Material Cost
      +
Manufacturing Cost
      +
Surface Treatment Cost
      +
Amortised Tooling Cost
      +
Overhead
      =
Total Unit Cost
```

---

# 📐 Cost Model

## Material Cost

```text
Material Cost
=
Material Price / kg × Part Mass
```

Material commodity prices are treated separately from regional processing costs.

---

## Manufacturing Cost

```text
Manufacturing Cost
=
Base Process Cost × Regional Labour Multiplier
```

---

## Surface Treatment Cost

```text
Surface Treatment Cost
=
Base Surface Process Cost × Regional Multiplier
```

---

## Tooling Cost

Tooling represents the fixed cost of moulds or dies and is amortised across production volume.

```text
Tooling Cost / Unit
=
Fixed Tooling Cost / Production Volume
```

---

## Total Unit Cost

```text
Subtotal
=
Material Cost
+
Manufacturing Cost
+
Surface Treatment Cost
+
Amortised Tooling Cost
```

Then:

```text
Final Unit Cost
=
Subtotal × Overhead Factor
```

The report describes this as a six-equation parametric cost model. :contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16}

---

# 📈 Cost Sensitivity Analysis

The cost model performs sensitivity analysis across multiple production volumes.

The resulting behaviour demonstrates the effect of tooling amortisation:

```text
Low Production Volume
        ↓
High Tooling Cost / Unit
        ↓
High Total Unit Cost
```

As production volume increases:

```text
Production Volume ↑
        ↓
Tooling Cost / Unit ↓
        ↓
Cost Curves Flatten
```

At higher production volumes, baseline material and processing costs increasingly dominate the total unit cost. :contentReference[oaicite:17]{index=17}

The report specifically analyses Aluminium Alloy, Titanium Alloy and CFRP to examine these volume-dependent cost transitions.

# ⚠️ Limitations

## 1. Aesthetic Subjectivity

Aesthetic perception is inherently influenced by:

- Individual preferences
- Culture
- Context
- Product category
- Design intent

Therefore, the aesthetic categories are computational representations rather than universal measurements.

---

## 2. AI Dependence

Vision-based recommendations depend on:

- Image quality
- Visual clarity
- Model interpretation
- Prompt structure
- Available database information

---

## 3. Cost Estimates

The cost model is intended for design-stage estimation.

Actual manufacturing cost can vary according to:

- Supplier
- Region
- Material market price
- Manufacturing facility
- Labour
- Tooling
- Production conditions
- Surface-treatment requirements

---

## 4. Database Expansion

The platform can be expanded with:

- More material grades
- More manufacturing processes
- Additional standards
- More cost information
- More surface-finish data
- Additional aesthetic mappings

---

## 5. Engineering Validation

AI-generated recommendations should be treated as decision-support information and should be validated by an appropriately qualified engineer before being used in safety-critical or production-critical applications.
